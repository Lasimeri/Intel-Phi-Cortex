## Findings

1. **Alignment, not capacity.** Every thread's block started at the same offset of a 2 MiB-aligned huge page. With many cores active, those identically placed blocks evict each other from the L2s: 50-58 % of lines missed every pass at 120 threads. With a different offset per thread, misses fall ~20x and the weights stay resident.
   - The structure involved is not identified (the distributed tag directory is a plausible candidate, unverified).
   - The effect and its fix are measured.
2. **Design rule for cortexd:** every column's weights get a distinct offset modulo 2 MiB. Use a prime-stride line offset, as `l2rpf2` does. Hot loops prefetch into L1 (`vprefetch0`) two rows ahead.
3. **Per-column cost at 120 threads:** 51.6 us median for a 210 KB F32 GEMV, vs 126 us when streamed fresh from GDDR. L2-resident columns are **2.4x** faster than GDDR-streamed ones at this size.
   - The remaining gap to the 14.0 us single-thread time = 2 threads per core (x2) + ~78 misses x ~0.3 us. This is arithmetic from the measured numbers.
4. ~~Per-token bound ~73 us~~ SUPERSEDED by run 7 below: it summed free-running per-thread medians. The lockstep step is 70.7 us median, giving a bound of ~85 us per token.

## Run 7: lockstep (added after review, 09:20)

cortexd waits for the slowest thread every step, so per-thread medians understate the step time. `lockpf2` and `lockrpf2` put a core2r barrier after every pass; thread 0 times each step from barrier exit to barrier exit.

Commands: `phi run /tmp/cortex/l2-gate /tmp/cortex/l2 3 1 120` and `... 3 1 118` (118 = cores 0-58 only). Raw: `results/s00f-l2-7-lockstep.log`, `data/l2f/`.

| lockrpf2, 118 threads (no core 59) | 69.6-69.9 | 124.5-157.0 | 72.5-75.3 |

- Core 59 costs ~1 us of median step (1.3 %) and the p99 ranges overlap: **keep 120 columns.**
- Corrected per-token bound (arithmetic from measured parts): 70.7 us step (GEMV + 1 barrier) + 2 x 6.8 us barriers + 0.7 us mailbox = **~85 us median, ~92 us mean**, i.e. ~11k-12k tok/s, before the head, softmax and host sampling.
